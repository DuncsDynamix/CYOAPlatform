# Gold Tap Discovery Questions

Goal: leave the conversation with **one named client, one named scenario, and one
named buyer** — a pilot Gold Tap would put their name to.

What we already know (don't ask — build on): Gold Tap trains the UK water industry
(leak detection, valve ops, water sampling, emergency supply, customer service);
they deliver formal certifications (National Water Hygiene, SHEA, Thames Water
schemes); their end-customers are utilities, so the deal shape is B2B2B with Gold
Tap's brand as the credibility asset; they already sell e-learning alongside
classroom delivery. From April: branding, SCORM/LMS delivery, and audit-grade
certification evidence were flagged as blockers on them selling Traverse-built
courses. This conversation should *test* those assumptions against a real deal,
not re-derive them. ★ = the questions that matter most.

## 1. Where's the live heat?

- ★ Which client has mentioned a training problem more than once this year — and
  what were their exact words for it?
- Which clients have an audit, accreditation renewal, or regulator attention coming
  in the next 6–12 months? (DWI, health & safety, insurer, EUSR/CABWI scheme audits…)
- Of your course catalogue, which ones are really about *judgment under pressure*
  rather than procedure recall — customer service? emergency supply? refusal-of-entry
  and doorstep situations? Where do delegates visibly struggle in the classroom?
- Has any client asked for something you currently can't deliver well — refresher
  practice between courses, remote assessment, scenario testing?

## 2. How judgment training happens today

- When a utility needs staff to handle difficult *situations* — an angry customer on
  a doorstep, a contamination scare, a stop-work call — what do you deliver today,
  and what does a day of it cost them?
- ★ What happens between annual certifications? Is there anything, or is it
  certify-and-forget until renewal?
- Where do clients grumble: cost, releasing staff off shift, pass rates that don't
  predict real performance, "e-learning that proves nothing"?

## 3. The evidence question — our differentiator; listen hardest here

- ★ When an auditor asks a client "how do you know this operative is competent?",
  what do they actually show — a certificate, a register, an LMS report? Is anyone
  embarrassed by that answer?
- Has a client ever had an incident where the person involved was "fully trained"
  on paper? What happened to how they think about training evidence afterwards?
- Would a client pay more per head for an *assessed scenario record* — this person
  faced a realistic doorstep/emergency situation and here's the rubric evidence —
  versus a completion certificate? Gut feel, and which client would say yes first.

## 4. Getting to one concrete scenario

- ★ Pick the most likely client: what is the one situation their people handle badly
  that costs the most? (Complaint escalations, CO/contamination discovery, refusal
  of entry, pressure to skip sampling steps, media contact during an incident?)
- Who at that client would sponsor a pilot — name and role? Who signs, who champions,
  who blocks?
- What would "this worked" look like to that sponsor after 90 days — what number or
  story do they repeat upwards?
- Which of your existing course materials, procedures, or incident case studies could
  seed the scenario's ground truth? (We build from their truth, not generic content.)

## 5. Delivery constraints — test the April blockers against a real deal

- ★ For a *pilot* with one client cohort: could delegates use a simple web link, or
  must it live inside the client's LMS from day one? (This decides whether SCORM
  export blocks the pilot or only the scale-up.)
- How important is it that the pilot carries Gold Tap branding rather than ours?
  (Assume very — but hear it priced: is it "nice" or "dealbreaker"?)
- Does the scenario record need to connect to a formal certification (NWH, SHEA) to
  be taken seriously, or can it stand alone as competence evidence in a pilot?
- Any client IT constraints we'd hit — locked-down devices, no audio on site, shared
  terminals in depots?

## 6. Commercials and Gold Tap's position

- ★ Does Gold Tap want to *sell* this (white-label, margin on top) or *introduce* it
  (referral)? What's the difference to you commercially?
- What price feels credible to a utility for: a bespoke scenario pack? per-operative
  per-year access? a quarterly incident exercise for a control-room team?
- What would Gold Tap need — commercially or reputationally — to put your name on a
  pilot? What's the thing that would make you *not* risk it?
- Who do we displace in the client's mind: their e-learning vendor, a consultancy,
  or "nothing, this budget doesn't exist yet"? (The last one is the hard sell.)

## 7. The demo moment (do it in the meeting, not after)

Lead with **"Discoloured: A Water Quality Event"** (`/scenario/goldtap-water-quality-event`)
— it runs under Gold Tap branding, in his industry's exact vocabulary: a
discolouration cluster after a contractor repair, sampling-before-flushing, the
duty manager phone call, the DWI notification decision, and the doorstep with a
frightened parent. The utility is fictional (Medway Water) so no real client is
depicted failing — offer to reskin per client. Then, if time, the Fernbrook
safeguarding scenario to show the same pattern travelling to any duty-of-care
sector. Voice on. Then ask only two questions:

- ★ Which of your clients did you think of while playing that — and which *situation*?
- What would have to change before you'd show it to them under the Gold Tap name?

The answer to the second question is the real product roadmap.
